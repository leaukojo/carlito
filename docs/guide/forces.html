Forces on a vehicle

Everything a vehicle does comes from pushes and pulls: gravity, the ground, the tyres, the air and the water. This page draws them all at once, for each of the seven kinds of vehicle, and lets you change the speed, the slope, the wind and the controls to see how each force answers.

An illustration, not the physics

The arrows come from a simplified copy of the game's formulas, worked out for one instant. They use the game's own figures for each vehicle: its weight, grip, engine, brakes and drag. The game itself works wheel by wheel, 60 times a second, with springs, slip and momentum, so its numbers differ from these. What this page leaves out is listed below.

How to read it

  • There are two views, from above and from the side. The vehicle faces right in both, so the side view shows its right-hand side. The side view draws only the near wheels. The arrows of the far-side wheels are drawn faint, a little offset.
  • Each arrow starts where its force acts and points the way the force pushes. Forces are measured in newtons: one newton is roughly the weight of a small apple.
  • All the arrows in a view share one scale, shown by the bar in its bottom corner: an arrow twice as long is a force twice as big. The longest ticked force in the view sets its scale, so the two views can have different bars.
  • Weight is usually the biggest force by far, and the ground's push answers it. Untick both to zoom in on the small forces, such as rolling resistance. A force too small to draw at the scale shows as a dot.
  • Point at an arrow or a force's name, or tap it, to read what that force is. A tyre asked for more than its grip gets a red ring in the view from above.
  • Tick Net force to see what is left when every push is added up. The box under the views says what it does: speed the vehicle up, slow it down, or turn it.

The seven vehicles

Each choice uses one body from the game and its figures. The Load slider means something different for each, and only where the game has a load:

  • Car: the sedan, which drives its front wheels. The cars carry no load.
  • Truck: the garbage truck. Its load is the refuse in the hopper, up to about 5 tonnes when full, which the game adds to the truck's weight at its centre of mass.
  • Tractor: the standard tractor. Its load is an implement raised on the three-point hitch, up to the harrow's 1.5 tonnes, hanging behind the rear axle. Watch it take weight off the front wheels.
  • Boat: the speedboat. Boats have no brakes and carry no load.
  • Plane: the plane, rolling along the runway until its wings carry its weight.
  • Drone: the standard drone. The throttle slider becomes its climb stick, and its load is a parcel on the hook, up to 4 kg.
  • Train: a locomotive and four wagons. The game's train ignores the wind.

What this page leaves out

  • Time. The page shows one instant. Nothing springs, rolls, pitches or builds up speed; the readout only says which way the vehicle would go.
  • Tyre detail. A tyre here grips up to its coefficient of friction times the weight on it, and no further. The game's tyres also lose a little grip per kilogram as the load rises (load sensitivity), and build their forces from slip. Past the limit the page uses the game's figure: a locked or spinning tyre keeps 0.8 of its best grip, and ABS or traction control hold it at the best.
  • Turning. The wheels point along a circle set by the wheelbase and the steering angle, the bicycle angle. The side force is shared between the axles by where the weight sits, and the load moves to the outside wheels. The game's tyres turn with a slip angle; here they do not.
  • The engine. The drive force is the throttle's share of the most the best gear can give at that speed. There are no gear changes, pedal maps or engine braking, and at low speed the engine is allowed to sit at its strongest while the clutch or converter slips.
  • Brakes. Moving, the brakes push at their full strength for the pedal; at a standstill they push only as hard as it takes to hold. No heat, no fade and no hill-start assist.
  • The ground. Dry asphalt only. In the game each kind of ground changes grip and rolling resistance: see Ground and tyre types.
  • The air and the water. A steady wind with no gusts, and still water with no current: see Wind, current and conditions.
  • The boat. Its four buoyancy points share the weight equally; in the game the bow or stern point pushes harder as the boat pitches. The water's sideways drag is left out.
  • The plane. The wings stay level, with no nose-up angle and no flaps.
  • The drone. The four rotors share the thrust equally; the game's mixer sets each one apart to tilt and turn it.
  • The train. The cars move as one, so the couplers pull steadily; the game's couplers are springs that stretch and settle.
  • The tractor's load. Every implement is placed where the harrow's weight sits when raised.

Try it in the game

Try it in the game: compare the page with the car

  1. Open sloppyCAN beside the game and pick the sedan on Flatland.
  2. Get up to about 100 km/h, then brake as hard as you can and watch accLong go negative.
  3. On this page pick the car, set the speed to 100 km/h, the throttle to 0 and the brake to 100 %. The readout's "slowing down at" is the page's version of the same number.
  4. The two are close but not the same: the game's tyres lose a little grip under load, and its nose dips as it brakes. That gap is the part this page leaves out.
