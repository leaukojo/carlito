### Physics derivations and figures

**Differentials.** Declared in `gen_kenney_vehicles.gd`:

| Body | Declaration |
| --- | --- |
| `suv`, `suv-luxury` | 3:1 Torsen-type centre |
| `race-future` | the same centre, plus a 2.5 rear LSD |
| `race` | 2.5 rear LSD |
| `hatchback-sports` | 2.5 helical front LSD |
| `tractor-kenney` | open rear with a lock; rigid MFWD |
| everything else, semis and trailers included | open |

A biasing diff caps the transfer at `|T_in|/2 · (b−1)/(b+1)`, so the slow side takes at most `b`×
the fast. Against all-open diffs, the biasing centres launch faster (most on `race-future`), the
axle LSDs change nothing in a straight launch (both wheels turn at one speed), and top speeds do
not move.

A biasing diff shows up as power oversteer, so the corner pass holds speed with a PI pedal
(`measure_vehicles.gd`): the diffs move peak lateral g by a few hundredths (bang-bang pulses spun `race`
at an implausible reported g).

**Open-diff peel.** Each `RayWheel` integrates its own spin, and the grip curve falls past 0.12
slip. Under an equal split, the wheel that slips further grips less and spins further still, so
the pair delivers 2× the weaker wheel's grip. That is real open-diff behaviour, and it is why an
open body sticks floored on bumps while a feathered pedal crosses. The tractor's MFWD is rigid, as a real one is: engaged, it multiplies mud gradeability
(`docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Gradeability), at the cost of real driveline wind-up in tight turns. The 2WD tractor floored in
mud climbs 16.7 % (9.5 deg) against a 17.7 deg traction ceiling. Held at the peak (`tc`) it climbs
25.0 % (14.1 deg), so the floored shortfall is the peel, not the tractor. A real 2WD tractor bogs in
mud too; MFWD and the diff lock are the answer. The same peel limits it ploughing (`docs/tractor.md`
§ Draft, hitch, linkage). The `tractor_mud` challenge sets that lesson on level 1's wallow, but on
its line (`measure_rough -- course=tractor_mud`) every driveline reaches the far zone floored in
a sixth of its par (`par_s` in `tractor_mud.tres`), the open 2WD with a driven wheel past 0.3 slip
on 16 % of the ticks: the par separates none of them.

**Wheel radius.** Each axle runs its drawn tyre's radius (`GroundDriveSpec.axle_radius`): 0.36 m (the kit
wheel at `KIT_SCALE`) on every Kenney body, semi and trailer, 0.44 / 0.66 m on the tractor, its `final_drive` and inboard
brakes quoted at the rear. `WheelDrive` gears a wheel `n = r / drive_radius()` to the shaft: MFWD turns the fronts 1.5×.

**Contact.** Each wheel sweeps a cylinder of its tyre's radius and width down its travel from full
bump (a start inside something reads full bump); the normal is the face's, or radial at an edge.
At a square step (`kerb`, 12 / 20 cm) a tyre first touches the face ahead of its hub, partway up its
arc (a disk touches a little further out, steeper); a crawl's largest tick binds no clamp and
peaks at a gentle g. A ray jumps the whole step in one tick (several g, clamp-bound) and, tilted
with the body, pushes sideways off the face; parallel rays strand the trucks on 20 cm. Cost,
`semi` + `box`: the cast costs an order of magnitude over the ray, natively and on web (`kerb`,
`docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Measure-tool run lines).

The sweep bisects to 1/256 of the travel, so the hub's drop is read between its last free stop and
its first touching one: `settled_drop` backs the touching stop out by the rest contact's overlap.
Without it the hub flickers across a step at rest, which the dampers turn into load spikes (a
semi's steer wheel lost about a kilonewton at launch). With the face micrometres past a stop on a
pitched body, the sweep can call that stop touching where the rest test still reads it clear, more
often far from a large collider's centre (float32 precision; `measure_vehicles`' strip is one 6 km
box). That stop is a free one, so the bracket moves a step on and the contact is read there.
Settling on the stop before it puts a whole step into one tick's compression: on a semi's steer
axle at a steady pull, a one-tick swing of about ten points of its parked load, up and back
(`test_wheel_spin`'s hub-on-a-sweep-stop case; the trace's `contact` lines).

**Spin step.** Tyre force is huge next to the wheel's own inertia (`I / r²` is 207 kg on the
tractor's rear, against its 1750 kg corner mass), so an explicit step rings at the tick rate. Against a
480 Hz reference, capping the reaction term gave +20 % top speed on the car and +41 % on the
tractor. The brake rides the same step: applied after it, a held brake needs the tyre to carry
`1 + k` times its torque (k is small on a sedan at speed, large on a trailer wheel), so any
pedal past ~30 % locks; measured that way, the sedan at 30 % stopped with a wheel past 0.3
slip for seconds.

**Following the body.** Solved for spin with the road speed frozen, the step reads each tick's
change in road speed as slip it must buy back, and a body that accelerates costs its wheels
`I * a * k / r`. That is about `dv per tick / slip velocity` of the torque: a sizeable share of a full brake
mid-stop, more at low speed. So the step follows the body's last-tick velocity change at the centre
of mass, in proportion `k / (1 + k)`. Against the same runs at 480 Hz it lands 0-100 times and
full-pedal stops within 4 %, where spin alone ran 6-11 % long.

The follow reads the centre of mass, never the contact. The contact also carries the pitch that
the tyre's own force rocks in one tick, and following it rings the body at the tick rate. Below
twice the slip floor the follow fades out (there it rings too, and buys nothing), which is the
launches' remaining 1-3 %.

**One-tick caps and the slip floor.** Each tyre force is capped at what cancels its slip velocity
in one tick, on the mass the body's tensor gives each contact (`RayWheel.size_cap_masses`): the
patch velocity is read about the COM, and on `corner_mass` alone a crawl rings (slip 0.15 at rest)
and a parked `semi` slides 5 m off its hand brake on 16.7 deg. The body's slip floor
(`slip_floor_for`) keeps the grip curve inside those caps, so a crawl reaches the full budget and
the cap takes nothing off any launch (`doc=accel`'s tick cap 0.00); per-wheel floors held
`suv-luxury` at 66.8 % against 81.0 %. Guard: `test_wheel_spin` (`contact_masses`, `slip_floor_for`).

**Braking.**

The stops at each pedal: `docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Braking.

A converter body's part-pedal stop is a real automatic's: the foot brake releases the lock-up
(`Drivetrain.braking`, as a TCU does on braking), so the brakes never slow the crank through a
lower gear, and the open converter passes the crank's friction back as overrun. A `sedan` 30 %
stop traces only the crank's friction torque from speed down to the last downshift. Below the
turbine's idle speed it creeps against the brake for the last stretch. `garbage-truck` stops at 30 %: its
idle creep (`docs/drivetrain.md` § Converter) sits under what that pedal asks of its brake hardware (`WheelDrive.brake_application`). The clutch bodies
keep their clutch closed, so their engine braking includes the friction: `race`'s 30 % stop is the
shortest at that pedal. Full pedal, on ABS, is unaffected.

Mean g is speed over time, so it reads under the peak. Full pedal asks the brake hardware's
whole capacity (§ Brake hardware), more than any tyre gives, so every ABS body stops on ABS and
`race` (no ABS) locks its wheels: it stops shorter at 60 % pedal than at 100 %, as a real non-ABS
car does. The pedal is anchored per body (`WheelDrive.brake_application`): half pedal asks
`BRAKE_HALF_PEDAL_G` of every body and full pedal its whole capacity, so 60 % reads
about the same across the table whatever the hardware (`docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Braking), and a body with more capacity is gentler in
the first third and keeps the rest for the end of the travel. The semi anchors on its coupled load
(EBS load sensing, `WheelDrive.brake_load_kg`), so the 32 t rig answers the pedal as a car does.
ABS reads the hub's speed off the ground too: the `sedan`'s rears leave the road briefly each
in a full-pedal stop near the tyre's peak g, and keep turning there instead of landing
locked. Steady transfer alone unloads them only at `g · rear share · L / h` (computed, on the
sagged COM height), well past the tyre's peak, so the lift is the stop's pitch dynamics: the dive
and its lean (`docs/handling.md` § Pitch). It stays, as honest for the footprint, and so does the 87 % front brake
split (`brake_bias_front` 0.873). No other road body lifts. The tractor (no ABS; rear brakes, the pedal engages MFWD) stops
in 5.4 m, skidding for under a second.

**Both pedals.** Floored in D1 against the full foot brake, traction control off (it would hold
the drive back itself), `STALL_HOLD_S` (`measure_vehicles -- <variant> 30 stall`: a converter
held on its stall speed, a clutch slipping on its launch target). Every
body moves under 5 cm:
- 0.000 m: `sedan`, `hatchback-sports`, `suv`, `suv-luxury`, `taxi`, `race-future`,
  `tractor-kenney`, `delivery`, `delivery-flat`, `ambulance`, `garbage-truck`, `firetruck`;
- 0.3-1.9 cm, a driven wheel turning slowly against its brake while the other axle's brakes hold
  the body (a brake-stand burnout): `sedan-sports` (1.9), `race` (1.3), `police` (1.2),
  `pickup-flat` (0.8), `van` (0.7), `semi`, `semi-conventional`
  (0.6 each), `pickup` (0.4).

On a body with both axles driven the brakes are solved with the differentials
(`WheelDrive._couple_differentials`, `Differential.braked_coupling_torque`): a held wheel stays
held unless the torque a diff must pass it beats its brake, so the body holds on its brake total.
`suv-luxury`'s rear brakes (820 Nm a wheel) are under their own drive (~990 Nm); its Torsen centre
carries the excess to the fronts' spare brake. The tractor brakes only its rears; the pedal engages
MFWD, and the rigid shaft carries the fronts' drive into the rears' spare brake (34 kNm in total
against 23.6 kNm of gear 1 at stall). The static check is `BrakeSizing.stall_hold_margin`, and
`test_vehicle_catalog` holds every body to it. On the heavy vans (a 470 N·m engine through 15.6:1)
it reads unbounded on `delivery` and `delivery-flat`, whose driven rears' brakes alone outhold the
drive, and 43.7 on `ambulance`.

*The solve order* (`WheelDrive._couple_differentials`), one pass a tick, each coupling solved off
the wheels' own spin steps with every wheel's holds inside it:
1. The gearbox output. In P with the pawl in, a lock of the output to the case: the driven wheels'
   mean spin coupled to a ground of no compliance (an open diff downstream still lets its outputs
   counter-rotate, as a real one does in P). In D or R, the crank: an output with its own spin and
   compliance behind the controller's capacity, or behind the converter's law against the driven
   wheels' settled mean (`Drivetrain.couple`). Either torque sizes a biasing diff's capacity.
2. The centre, whenever both axles are driven (rigid for MFWD, else `centre_diff_bias`).
3. Each axle, its capacity sized off the torque it received; unbounded on the rear under the lock.
4. Every wheel settles (`RayWheel.settle_spin`): a braked wheel stays held unless the torque a
   diff must pass it beats its brake; a TC-held one stays at its ceiling unless the diff passes it
   more than its drive share.

COMPROMISE: one pass. An axle coupling between wheels of unequal compliance moves that axle's mean
after the centre balanced it (`race-future`'s rear LSD, the tractor's lock under MFWD); exact needs
each axle solved inside the centre's solve. Likewise a biasing centre or axle moves the driven
wheels' mean after the gearbox output settled against it (the pawl: `LOCK_STUCK_SPIN`); a crank
above a rigid one (MFWD, the rear lock) is solved nested (`WheelDrive._rigid_downstream`).

`brake_override` stays as the modelled brake-throttle override every family carries.

### Tyres and surfaces

A tyre is a **fitment** (the body's, on `GroundDriveSpec`: `mu_long` / `mu_lat` as the stock tyre
on dry asphalt, the grip curve, both load laws, radius, `rolling_resistance` on a hard road) times
a **class** (`TyreClass`, `src/vehicles/tyres/*.tres`, swappable in the garage): `dry` over the
reference all-season, a `surface_grip` factor per `Surface`, `crr_factor`. A class shared by a
1.1 t car and an 8 t truck cannot carry `cornering_peak_load` (`PKY2 * Fz0 / ref` over the body's
own corner load), so the load laws stay fitment. Grip on surface `s` is `spec mu x (dry_c /
dry_stock) x Surface ref_grip(s) x surface_grip_c(s)`: the stock on dry asphalt is the spec bit for
bit, and one factor per surface cancels in `BalanceModel`'s front/rear ratio. Brakes, the brake
split and peak slip (0.12, which `ABS_SLIP`, `TCS_SLIP` and the slip floor derive from) stay the
body's (§ Brake hardware; an ABS following a class's own peak: § Rejected designs). Surface drag is
tyre-blind. The fitted class is a session choice per variant (`UserSettings.tyres`,
`Boot._fit_tyre` on every spawn, the stock in a challenge, kept through a respawn; towed bodies keep
theirs). Surfaces: `docs/systems.md` § Level framework; figures: `docs/sources.md`; measured:
`docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Tyre classes by surface (`tyre=` on every measure tool).

### Brake hardware

Each body declares its brakes as a real hardware class, never derived from the tyre or from each
other (`tools/gen_kenney_vehicles.gd` baselines and variants; the semis' spec headers). Only the
axle split is derived: `_front_brake_share` proportions the foot brake by each axle's load-scaled
grip in a tyre-limited stop. `BrakeSizing` is the arithmetic the generator reports and
`test_vehicle_catalog` asserts:
- the foot brake is at least 1.15x the stock tyre, so it can lock, and past every other class its
  fit takes (`taxi` on Summer keeps 1.14x);
- it holds gear 1 at converter stall (§ Both pedals);
- the handbrake locks the static rear wheel and parks the laden body on 20 % (UN R13-H, to
  3.5 t) or 18 % (R13). Because it locks, the parking grade is the rear tyres' (`park_grade`,
  facing either way on the slope's weight transfer): 32-46 % on the road cars and vans (58-62 on
  the racers), 32 % on `garbage-truck` and 36 % on `firetruck`, 45 % on the tractor;
- a pawl outholds its driven tyres.

Spring brakes (the trucks' handbrake column) sit under the service brake on the same drum; a
truck whose air drains cannot drive away because the box drops to N (`Drivetrain.drive_inhibit`).
Every trailer's service brake is apportioned to its tractor: a full demand brakes its bogie load at
the deceleration the tractor's brakes make on its coupled load (`box_spec.tres`), so neither end
pushes the other.

**P (park).** Gear byte 0xFE, a value RAMN never sends. On a gearbox with a pawl
(`park_pawl_torque` > 0) it engages P; on one without, N. Below `WheelDrive.PAWL_ENGAGE_SPEED`
(1 m/s) the pawl locks the gearbox output to the case, and above it the pawl ratchets over the
teeth and holds nothing. The lock is the first coupling `_couple_differentials` solves. It is
`Differential.braked_coupling_torque` with the driven wheels on one side and a ground of no
compliance on the other, rated at the pawl times the driven wheels, and its torque reaches them
equally through the diffs, as a drive torque does. The driven wheels are held only through their
diffs:
- the wheels of an open axle (or an open centre's two axles) still turn against each other, so a
  car in P with one driven wheel on ice slides away;
- a diff lock or the tractor's MFWD shaft ties the wheels it couples.

The diffs solve after the lock and move the driven wheels' mean by what their compliances differ
(the one pass: 7.5e-4 rad/s on `suv-luxury` parked on 16 %). So the whole driveline counts as held
(`RayWheel.hold_by_lock`, the patch spring on) only when the lock carries less than its rating and
every locked wheel settles under `LOCK_STUCK_SPIN` (0.01 rad/s, 3.5 mm/s at a car's tread). A wheel
an open diff counter-rotates off lost grip turns at rad/s. The `pawl` columns of the park table
below hold P alone, with no brake.

**Handbrake turn.** A full handbrake at `HB_SPEED` with a `HB_STEER` step held `HB_HOLD_S`
(`measure_vehicles.gd`; `measure_vehicles -- <variant> 30 handbrake`; `sedan`, `police`,
`hatchback-sports`, `suv`, `race`; 2026-10-15): the rears lock for under a second and the body
turns about a half turn (integrated off the yaw rate, so a spin would read past it), ending
backwards: a J-turn. On the steer alone it turns far less. The lock does it: the combined-slip tyre
gives a locked wheel no side force.

### Holding at rest: static friction

Below `LOW_SPEED_FLOOR` the slip force is linear in the contact's slide. That makes it a damper,
zero at zero slide, so on its own a held wheel holds a grade only by sliding: ~1 m/min on 10 %
for every braked body. `RayWheel` adds a contact-patch spring beside it (`stick_step`,
`stick_stiffness`, `stick_saturate`, `friction_ellipse`), and the slip force acts as the spring's
damper:
- **Deflection.** The patch's deflection integrates its slide and decays per metre rolled over
  `STICK_RELAX`. That is a relaxation length, set so the rolling spring's steady force equals the
  grip curve's linear rise. Along the wheel it acts only while the wheel's spin is held: the brake
  (foot, handbrake or spring brakes) stopped it with torque to spare, or the park pawl holds the
  driveline still (§ P (park)), to the pawl's rating on top of the brake. An unbraked wheel rolls
  down a grade, and a free wheel's few kilograms of spin inertia would ring a spring at 60 Hz. The
  test is last tick's settle (`RayWheel.settle_spin`), where the brake is solved with the
  differentials.
- **Brake limit.** A held wheel's longitudinal force is capped at what its brake holds against
  its drive and what the differentials pass it, each with its sign (`held_force_range`: a creep up
  the grade widens the hold). Past that the body slides at the brake's
  Coulomb limit while the wheel stays held. If the wheel were let turn at that edge, it would
  flicker between held and free, and every catch would rewind the patch from zero on a moving body
  (measured: a 10 % creep at 89 % of the handbrake's capacity).
- **Stiffness.** The friction budget is reached at `STICK_DEFLECTION` (2 cm, ~175 kN/m on a car
  corner), capped at `STICK_TICK_STIFFNESS * mass / dt^2` (one of the 60 Hz clamps).
  On each axis the mass is `min(corner_mass, ...)` of the body's tensor figure for that
  contact (`RayWheel.size_cap_masses`).
- **Saturation.** The deflection is held on the friction ellipse's edge, so past the budget the
  spring carries the whole budget and the body slides at the friction limit rather than creeping.
  The sum with the slip force stays inside the ellipse.
- **Fade.** The spring fades in below `STICK_SPEED` (0.5 m/s) of ground speed, so driving,
  braking and cornering figures above a crawl never see it.

A held body winds the spring up by `STICK_DEFLECTION * tan(a) / mu` and then stays
put; without it every braked body creeps 1.0-1.5 m per minute on 10 %. Every handbrake holds 30 % (§ Brake hardware). Guard: `test_wheel_spin`
(`test_a_braked_wheel_holds_at_rest_on_a_grade`).

Side effects:
- **Grade.** Climbs near the limit run at a crawl under `STICK_SPEED`, so the spring lifts them a
  bisection step or more (`pickup` 53.5 to 54.2 %).
- **Rough ground.** The `sedan` and `pickup` wedge on the chassis in the asphalt 60 cm ditch (`b`;
  `docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Rough and soft ground).

The parked drift on 10 % and 30 %: `docs/vehicle_measurements.md` § Parking.

### Rejected designs

Do not re-propose these without new evidence:
- `FreeBodyDrive` / `HullBuoyancy`: boat, plane and drone drag share no shape, and buoyancy has one
  consumer.
- `VehicleSpec.air_drive` / `.water_drive`: the vehicle node is already the single home
  (`test_boat_variants` guards the boat's recipe copy).
- A polymorphic `spec.drive`: retires no `null` guard and adds a downcast to each.
- Splitting `Drivetrain` into gearbox and rpm: the gearbox is load-bearing on every family.
  COMPROMISE: the wheel-less bodies run an engine model nothing observes.
- Unifying the four reservoirs (truck air, train brake pipe, drone pack, fuel): four different
  models, two of which gate.
- Merging `TowedBody.Consumer` with `ImplementBase.Connection`: different sets (see the enum
  headers).
- Torque-curve tails on the six curves ending at `(redline, 0)` (the heavy vans, the Kenney trucks,
  `tractor-kenney`): they would move tuned top speeds for nothing.
- Open-diff friction (the open diff is an ideal 1.0): bodies that should fight one-wheel peel
  declare an LSD.
- Fixing the floored 2WD tractor in mud: it is the open diff's peel (§ Open-diff peel).
- Shipping converter torque multiplication before the brake-class review (`docs/drivetrain.md` § Converter); never a
  bigger brake to make a body hold.
- Auto-respawn on overturn (`base_vehicle.gd`).
- A cycling or peak-following ABS/TC: every tyre class shares the 0.12 peak, so `ABS_SLIP` /
  `TCS_SLIP` hold it ideally. Revisit when ABS/TC activity becomes a bus signal beyond
  `trailer_abs`, or a class gets its own peak slip.
- An analog bypass of the steering taper: it applies to every input source, as speed-sensitive
  steering does, and `test_a_steering_taper_never_out_limits_the_tyres` keeps 1.5x the usable lock.
  Revisit only if analog input must reach a lock the tyres cannot use.
- Drawing the heavy vans' twin rears: the kit tyre (0.42 m) on their 1.26 m rear track puts the
  inner pair at the centreline. They draw one tyre per corner over `rear_twin_tyres` physics.
- A predicted-slip or per-tick force-change cap instead of the slip floor (§ One-tick caps): on a
  one-contact pitch model the first alternates the force 0 / 2x, the second limit-cycles.
