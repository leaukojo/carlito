# Vehicles

The vehicle framework and the four families that carry one bus each. Truck/trailer/tractor (a
second machine on a second network) are in `docs/heavy_vehicles.md`; shared plumbing (contract,
input, telemetry, dashboard, bridge, lamps, shell, levels) is in `docs/systems.md`. Rules a
session editing vehicle code must not break: `src/vehicles/CLAUDE.md`, plus `drone/CLAUDE.md` and
`train/CLAUDE.md`.

## Vehicle framework

`src/vehicles/base/`:

### Specs and the variant catalog

- `VehicleSpec` is the wheeled vehicle's drive tuning in one `.tres`; new vehicle = new spec +
  scene. Feel tuning is data-only: edit spec numbers, keep the input-hierarchy test green (brake >
  peak drive > handbrake; handbrake holds only below ~30% throttle). All seven families read:
  body, the whole drivetrain group, `steer_speed` (the axis slew — moves a rudder and a drone's
  yaw too), lamps, `trailer_bus_equipped`. Free bodies are tuned elsewhere: boat/drone/plane carry
  15/18/34 tuning `@export`s on the vehicle NODE (plane also declares a `ground_drive` here —
  three undriven, braked, steered wheels are a real ground drive). The boat is tuned in two
  places: `plane.tscn`/`drone.tscn` override none of those exports, while each `boat-*.tscn`
  overrides ~9 from `tools/gen_boat_variants.gd`'s recipe. Change one without the other and the
  next regen silently undoes it.
- `GroundDriveSpec` is the wheeled half of that tuning, an embedded `[sub_resource]` on
  `spec.ground_drive`: wheels, driveline flags, suspension, tires, brakes, road resistance, and
  the steering lock (`max_steer_deg` / `min_steer_frac` / `steer_falloff_speed`). Pure data, no
  methods. Car, truck, tractor and the plane declare a real one; boat, drone and train declare
  none — those specs carry no wheel field, `BaseVehicle` builds no `WheelDrive`. It is optional,
  so every reader outside the wheeled path is null-guarded; embedded rather than an external
  `.tres` because a `.tres` is a bake input wherever it lives.
- `VehicleCatalog` (`src/vehicles/vehicle_catalog.gd`) is the static registry of vehicle
  *variants* (hand-built vehicles + Kenney car-kit bodies + Watercraft-pack boats) mapped onto
  contract *families* (car/truck/tractor/boat/drone/plane/train). The family drives bridge
  marshaling, dashboard cluster and spawn filtering; a variant carries its own spec (boats also
  their own hull/buoyancy knobs), so it drives differently but signals identically. Generated by
  `tools/gen_kenney_vehicles.tscn` and `tools/gen_boat_variants.tscn` (deterministic, destructive-
  by-run; re-run after a scale/feel change). V (or touch NEXT) cycles variants within the current
  family via `Level.set_vehicle`; `GameState.current_variant` tracks it. What hangs off the back
  is a separate axis on its own key: E (or touch ATTACH) duck-types `cycle_implement()` on the
  active vehicle, so the tractor's implement / semi's trailer cycle without changing the body.
  ATTACH hides where the vehicle tows nothing. Tested in `tests/test_vehicle_catalog.gd`. The
  contract never sees variants.

### Drivetrain, rev limiter, governor

- `Drivetrain` is pure math: engine torque curve, gear ratios, RAMN gear byte (0x00 = N, 0x01..
  0x06 = D1-D6, 0xFF = R), real RPM (wheel speed through the ratio with idle/redline clamps), auto-
  shift. Tested in `tests/test_drivetrain.gd`. A bridge gear byte bypasses auto-shift exactly.
  The rev limiter judges the unclamped crank speed the wheels impose (`wheel_engine_rpm`), never
  the published `rpm`, which is clamped to `[idle, redline]` and settles an epsilon short by lerp
  — a cut gated on it could never fire (arithmetic in `src/vehicles/CLAUDE.md` § Drivetrain and
  brakes). `limiter_cut` is its own predicate on the raw value and lands on `applied_throttle`
  like the governor does, so telemetry sees the cut too. `engine_torque` is just the curve; the
  limiter is what stops it.
  The road-speed governor (`spec.speed_limit_kmh`, 0 = ungoverned, J1939 SPN 74) is the other
  reason a vehicle stops well short of its redline. `governor_scale` fades delivered throttle to
  zero across the last `GOVERNOR_BAND` m/s below the limit rather than cutting at it, unsigned so
  it governs reverse too. `Drivetrain.applied_throttle` (not the pedal) is what telemetry must
  read for engine load and fuel/coolant/battery; `governed_upshift` runs against the limiter so a
  limit below the next upshift speed can't strand the box a gear short. Declared on the wire as
  `speed_limit` (u8, 1 km/h/bit, 0-250), copied off the spec once at spawn. Range-less on purpose:
  a readout line, not a bar. Unflavored: four of nine shipped limits sit on the car family, which
  doesn't speak J1939, so the SPN is a naming reference only.

### Wheels and the chassis

- `RayWheel` is one ray per wheel: spring-damper suspension + slip-based tires. The 60 Hz
  stability clamps live here (damper ≤ one-tick reversal, suspension force cap, low-speed slip
  floors + one-tick lateral force cap). Tire grip scales by painted ground: each contact samples
  `HeightmapTerrain.grip_at()` (`BaseVehicle` collects the level's grip terrains once, duck-typed);
  the multiplier rides `mu_long`/`mu_lat`, and the clamps are untouched since they cap by
  momentum, independent of mu. The terrain used is the one whose surface is nearest the contact
  and within `SURFACE_GRIP_REACH`: a conformed road deck reads the paint beneath it, while a
  bridge/ramp passing over a painted patch keeps neutral grip.
- `BaseVehicle` (RigidBody3D) consumes `InputRouter.get_vehicle_input()` only, runs
  wheels/drivetrain, publishes telemetry, applies lamps, plays the horn on the rising edge
  (source-agnostic). Wheel-less-safe: no `ground_drive` means no `WheelDrive` at all. Two virtual
  seams for subclasses: `_make_telemetry()` (telemetry factory, used in `_ready`) and
  `_tick_extras(input, delta)` (empty in base, runs last in `_physics_process` so drivetrain RPM +
  telemetry motion are current). Subclasses never fork `_physics_process`.

### Drag, downforce and drift

- Road resistance (`VehicleMath.aero_drag` / `rolling_drag` / `road_resistance`, applied by
  `WheelDrive._apply_resistance` and `TowedBody.tick_towed`) sets a wheeled vehicle's top speed.
  Aero is `0.5 * rho * Cd*A * v^2` off `drag_area`; rolling resistance is `crr * N` off
  `rolling_resistance`, where N is the suspension force the springs reported this tick, not
  `mass * g`: an airborne wheel resists nothing, a loaded truck resists more, with no second
  number. One-tick clamped like every damper; neither term reads the mass. Every chassis is on
  `DAMP_MODE_REPLACE` at 0, driven or not — `physics/3d/default_linear_damp` is linear in v where
  real aero is quadratic and scales per-mass (on a coupled rig that alone plateaued the semi in
  gear 4). A trailer's drag area is a marginal in-the-wake figure (0.25-0.60 m² against 2.4 m² of
  frontal area). Boat/drone/plane declare neither term, running their own drag through
  `VehicleMath`. Cd*A on Kenney bodies is derived: `gen_kenney_vehicles` measures each body's AABB
  and takes `cd * FRONTAL_FILL * w * h`. Tested in `tests/test_vehicle_math.gd`;
  `tests/test_vehicle_catalog.gd` sweeps every variant for a declared pair.
- Aerodynamic downforce (`VehicleMath.aero_downforce`, `WheelDrive._apply_downforce`) is the same
  `0.5 * rho * C*A * v^2` off `downforce_area` (Cl*A), pushed down the body's up axis. Declared
  only by the two open-wheelers, from the recipe's `cl`. It is a force through the suspension, not
  a grip multiplier: springs compress, `RayWheel` applies mu to the bigger suspension force, and
  the cornering limit climbs with v² instead of sitting flat. It costs ride height
  (`test_vehicle_catalog` fails a `cl` that would bottom the springs) and the rolling resistance of
  the extra load, and is not gated on ground contact. Worth over 12 km/h of top speed on `race`: a
  slip tyre carrying more load makes the same drive force at less slip, and the drag cost was
  already priced into that body's `cd`.
- `ChaseCamera` follows `get_global_transform_interpolated()`. `LampSet` / `Horn`: see
  `docs/systems.md` § Lamps, horn & day/night.
- Arcade drift: `GroundDriveSpec.handbrake_grip` scales rear lateral grip while the handbrake is
  pulled. The hierarchy test caps `handbrake_torque` too low to lock the rears, so drift comes
  from the grip cut, not brake torque.

### Measuring a vehicle: `tools/measure_vehicles.tscn`

For the questions unit tests can't answer, needing a whole vehicle on the ground for a minute: how
fast is this thing, and does it go where it's pointed? Acceleration/top-speed is a dev report
(exits 0, read against the power balance); tracking is a CI gate. Game-mode tool scene (autoloads
+ a live physics step, which `--script` mode has neither):

```powershell
& $GODOT --headless --path . res://tools/measure_vehicles.tscn -- sedan-sports
& $GODOT --headless --path . res://tools/measure_vehicles.tscn -- all 45
& $GODOT --headless --path . res://tools/measure_vehicles.tscn -- all 45 track strict
```

First argument: a `VehicleCatalog` variant id (default `sedan-sports`) or `all` (every wheel-
driven variant — boat/drone/plane have no driven axle, need water/air not a strip). Second: per-
pass time cap in seconds; `coast`, `track`, `strict` are flags after it, any order.

The strip is a bare 40 × 6000 m `StaticBody3D`, `surface_grip` 1.0 (asphalt-equivalent). Two
passes, both with steering never touched:

#### The three passes

- Acceleration / top speed — 0-50/100/150/200 km/h, 0-60 mph, quarter mile, and settled top speed
  with the gear/rpm it lands in. Flags a vehicle that tops out below its tallest ratio. Top speed
  is settled, not exact: the run stops once it gains less than ~0.2% over three seconds. A capped
  pass reports the same way, so a too-slow vehicle prints a low top speed with no indication it
  was cut short (`sedan` needs ~38 s to reach 200 km/h; reported 213.8 km/h at `all 60` against
  235.1 with room to run). Check the power balance (engine kW at reported rpm vs
  `0.5*rho*Cd*A*v^3 + crr*m*g*v`) before trusting a figure, and raise the cap for slow ones.
- Straight-line tracking catches a chassis that quietly pulls. Respawned, accelerated to 60 km/h
  (launch transient excluded), pose latched as the ideal line; runs 200 m with zero steer
  measuring lateral offset and heading change. Thresholds 1.0 m and 1.0°; the twenty shipped
  wheel-driven variants measure 0.000 m for eighteen and 0.065 m at worst, so a FAIL means a real
  asymmetry (mistyped `wheel_positions` x, one-sided drive split, uneven brake torque). Falsified
  before being trusted: a deliberate 400 N lateral force reads 6.033 m and 2.825°.
  This pass is a CI gate (`tracking` job, `-- all 45 track strict`): `track` skips acceleration,
  `strict` exits nonzero on FAIL. ~5.5 min for twenty variants, parallel to `build`; `publish-dev`
  needs both.
- Coast-down (opt-in, `coast` flag) diagnoses why a vehicle plateaus low: cuts throttle at settled
  top speed, measures deceleration from resistance alone, separating a drive-force shortfall from
  a parasitic sink. Work out what the declared model owes first (`0.5 * rho * Cd*A * v^2 +
  crr * m * g` off the spec's own numbers); only the remainder is a sink worth hunting. Also
  reports chassis contacts (RayWheel is a raycast — any body contact here is the hull scraping; a
  window in the first second is the spawn drop, not a scrape).
  The force column reads against all-up mass. Against the chassis' own `spec.mass` it understated
  the semi's resistance fourfold: a resistance reading lower than the drag stand-in alone sent two
  sessions hunting a force sink that didn't exist. Header shows `8000 kg chassis, 32000 kg all up`
  wherever the two differ. There is no way to measure a bobtail tractor unit from here:
  `SemiTractor` couples its first trailer unconditionally a few ticks after spawn, so `-- semi` is
  always the 32 t combination.

#### The drone's own tool

The drone has its own tool, `tools/measure_drone.tscn` (`_wheel_driven_variants` skips every
chassis with no driven axle). No arguments; five passes on a flat strip in still air (~55 s):
hover collective and pack current, terminal climb/descent rate, time to lean and translate, hover
endurance figured two ways, one-motor-out (drops ESC1, reports how fast yaw diverges). The
translate figure is taken at the time cap (the pass can't run longer without breaching the
geofence), so a `residual` line prints speed still being gained per second; the headline is a
floor, not a top speed. Every figure is read off published telemetry (hover collective
reconstructed from the four `esc_rpm` through the mixer's `thrust ~ ω²` mapping), so a divergence
from the arithmetic `DroneVehicle`/`DronePower` state is a finding. It flies the craft by
registering as an InputRouter source, not by pressing Input actions: autoloads tick before scene
nodes, so an action pressed from a tool's `_physics_process` is already stale.

#### The `Engine.time_scale` trap

Do not try to make this faster with `Engine.time_scale`. Godot scales the delta handed to
`_physics_process` rather than running more iterations, enlarging the step and breaking the
locked-60-Hz rule. Measured: the default car's 0-100 goes 5.30 s → 6.40 s at `time_scale` 8. An
`all` run is genuinely several minutes of wall clock; background it.

## Boat

The water it floats on is a LEVEL node, not a vehicle one: `WaterSurface`/`WorldBounds` in
`docs/systems.md` § Water & world bounds.

- `BoatVehicle extends BaseVehicle` (`src/vehicles/boat/`) follows the tractor template: only the
  two seams (`_make_telemetry()` → `BoatTelemetry`, `_tick_extras` = buoyancy/drag/thrust/rudder),
  `respawn()` = `super()` + trim reset. Its spec declares no ground drive, so `axle_torque` from
  the drivetrain is discarded. Boat, drone and train reference `drivetrain` nowhere in their own
  files: they run the whole engine model at 60 Hz for the gear byte alone (`auto_shift` indexes
  the kept 6 `gear_ratios`; gear scale is `DEFAULT_ROAD_RADIUS`). Accepted compromise (`TODO.md`),
  not a design to copy. Boat node knobs (probes, float_depth, thrust, rudder, drag, prop/keel
  offsets) are `@export` on BoatVehicle, like the tractor's hitch knobs.
- Buoyancy = 4 probes with the RayWheel clamp discipline (hull/yaw damping through the shared
  `VehicleMath.damped_force`, also used by plane/drone dampers — RayWheel's own clamps are a
  different force shape and stay local to `RayWheel`): per-probe spring k is derived (`m*g /
  (probes * float_depth)` — floats by construction), damper clamped to the one-tick reversal
  impulse, total clamped `[0, max_probe_force_factor × weight share]`; hull drag/yaw damping use
  `damped_force` (may at most zero the velocity it opposes in one tick). Pure statics, tested in
  `tests/test_boat.gd`. Feel comes from the levers: thrust at `prop_offset` below COM = bow-up
  under throttle; lateral drag at `keel_offset` below COM = heel in turns; windage at
  `windage_offset` — a body-space POINT, not a scalar — above the COM = heel in a beam wind and
  aft of it = the bow weathercocking up into the wind.
- The boat is the third body to fly relative to `WindField` (`WindField.at(self)`, sampled once
  a tick like the drone's and the plane's). The above-waterline hull takes an anisotropic drag
  from the air-relative velocity (`windage_long`/`windage_lat`), the same shape as the hull drag
  with the wind subtracted — deliberately not `VehicleMath.air_damper`, whose `axis` masks
  WORLD space while fore-aft against athwartships is a body-frame split on a hull that yaws.
  Air is 1/800 the density of water, so the force is small and honest rather than dramatic; the
  visible payoff is the instruments. Level 6 is the only level with a `WindField`.
- **`boat-sail-a` sails.** `BoatSail` (`src/vehicles/boat/`) is a vehicle-level system in the
  `BoatAutopilot` shape — pure statics, the per-tick state on `BoatVehicle` — applying a lift/drag
  force at `sail_center` above the COM, resisted by the deep keel the hull already has
  (`keel_extra` 0.60). The polar is a **labelled honest model and it is the FLAT-PLATE pair**
  (`Cl = CL_MAX·sin 2a`, `Cd = CD_MIN + CD_STALL·(1 − cos 2a)/2`): a soft sail is a thin cambered
  plate rather than an airfoil, so that shape is right at the three points that matter (0 no lift,
  45 deg peak lift, 90 deg pure drag) and needs no stall branch. **Luffing is the one thing the
  model imposes**: below `LUFF_DEG` the lift term is zero, because a soft sail cannot hold camber
  at a small angle of attack — and that is what makes irons a real state rather than a slow one.
  - **The no-go zone is EMERGENT, not clamped.** Close to the wind the rig's side force runs
    several times its drive, and against `drag_lat` that is a leeway angle wide enough that the
    course made good stays well off the wind however high the bow points. Leeway is part of
    `linear_velocity`, so `apparent_wind` sees it and the loop closes on itself; the crab it
    leaves between `cog` and `heading` is the same one the tide makes, read on the same rose. A
    hull that points unrealistically high wants its `drag_lat` looked at, never a cutoff.
  - **The sheet is a LIMIT, not a position** (contract `sheet`, the **3** key or sloppyCAN, 0..1).
    A boom is a free-swinging spar: the wind lays it to leeward until either the sheet stops it or
    it lines up with the airflow, so travel is `min(sheet · sheet_max_deg, |awa|)` and hauling in
    is not the mirror of easing out — easing PAST the apparent wind angle luffs the sail rather
    than easing it further, which is why over-easing slows the boat. What the boom did comes back
    on `sail_angle` (out), the `nav_mode`/`nav_mode_actual` pair's shape. `sail_angle` is signed in
    the same rotational sense as `awa`, which is what makes `aoa = awa − sail_angle` true; a boom
    being an aft-pointing spar, a positive angle in that sense lays its far end to port, and since
    the boom is always to leeward the sign simply follows the wind's side. The GLB's own sail
    mesh swings with it (`sail_pivot`), so the boom on screen is the boom the force came from.
    Both the reading and the mesh are **ungated by the buoyancy**, like the wind instruments they
    are read from — a boom swings on the trailer, and one frozen at its last angle would be stale
    rather than still. Only the FORCE is gated, because a beached hull has no drag to oppose it.
  - `sheet` / `sail_angle` are **UNFLAVORED**, and for a reason neither `engine_hours` nor
    `pitch` has: **NMEA 2000 defines no sail PGN at all**, so any flavor would name a wire that
    does not exist. The `train` flavor is not the precedent — that one borrows practice and is
    still a flavor. `sail_angle` is also range-less, so nothing on the generated path draws it:
    it rides the readout line as SAIL because `Dashboard.READOUT_EXTRAS` names it.
  - **A rig is anatomy, not a family trait.** Contract signals key on the family, so all three
    boats declare both signals and the two powerboats publish a resting 0; what gates the SHEET
    button is `BoatVehicle.vehicle_capabilities()["sail"]`, i.e. `sail_area > 0`, the same shape
    as the garbage truck's `body_cmd`. `sail_area` 0 also skips the whole force block.
  - The re-tune this needed: `thrust_force` 500 N and `drag_long` 150 are **one pair** — 500 N is
    a ~5 hp auxiliary outboard on a 4.5 m, 500 kg hull and 150 N per m/s puts its top speed at the
    3.3 m/s that outboard makes. The old 2200 / 380 was a 40 hp outdrive against a hull three
    times too draggy to sail. `drag_lat`, `drag_yaw`, `rudder_torque`, mass and probe span did not
    move, so `boat_autopilot.gd`'s zeta table is untouched. The engine stayed rather than going to
    zero: it is how you leave the slipway and get out of irons, and it keeps BURN / OIL / TRIM /
    `engine_hours` / the gear byte honest on this hull instead of publishing dead numbers.
- **Level 6 is also the only level with water under the boat rather than beside it**: every
  other island's `Sea` at y=1 over a pan clamped to 0 is a 1 m column, so `depth` would read a
  constant. Level 6's is at y=6 (`gen_skyport.gd`'s `SEA_Y`, ceiling 6.6), and since the level
  is generated end to end the beach band, kill box, boat spawn and slipway derive from it.
- Below the waterline the boat swims in a second field: `CurrentField` (`src/levels/base/`), a
  SIBLING of `WindField` and not a subclass — same `Resource` shape, same flows-TOWARD compass
  convention, same duck-typed `at(node)` walk, sharing only `base_vector`, because a tide and a
  gust have no arithmetic in common. `set_deg`/`drift`/`tide_period_s`/`tide_offset_s` ride a
  **signed** sinusoid, so the stream floods, goes slack and ebbs back through the same
  `base_vector` with no branch. `Level` carries it beside `wind` (`current`, `current_vector()`,
  both off one `_env_time`), and F3 prints it on the line below the wind. **Hull drag is measured
  against the WATER, not the ground**: `through_water = linear_velocity - current` feeds the
  fore-aft and lateral terms and, through `v_long`, the rudder authority — so a boat stemming the
  tide at drift rate sits still over the bed with full steerage way, and one lying stopped is
  swept downstream. Yaw damping stays on the raw angular velocity: a uniform stream has no
  gradient across the hull and exerts no yaw moment. Still water (no `CurrentField`) leaves every
  term bit-identical to the pre-tide one, the regression `tests/test_current.gd` pins.
- `stw`/`sog`/`cog`/`current_set`/`current_drift` (PGNs 128259, 129026, 130577) all come out of
  one static, `BoatTelemetry.flow_toward`, which reads any horizontal flow as (speed, bearing it
  flows TOWARD) — `sog`/`cog` off the ground velocity, `stw` off `through_water`,
  `current_set`/`current_drift` off the tide itself. **`sog` is not a second name for
  `speed`/`kmh`**, which are the signed LONGITUDINAL component along the bow: a crabbing hull
  makes ground the bow is not pointing at, and the `cog`-minus-`heading` gap IS that crab angle.
  The set of a current is NOT inverted the way `twd` is — practice names a wind by where it comes
  from and a current by where it goes, so `true_wind` is `flow_toward` plus 180 and
  `current_set` is `flow_toward` bare. A zero-length flow has no bearing and reads 0, the
  sentinel `twd`/`awa` already use. `stw`/`sog`/`current_drift` carry no `range` like
  `aws`/`tws`; `cog`/`current_set` are ranged like `twd`, so they are bars.
- `depth` (PGN 128267 Water Depth) is the same seabed the hull collides with, read a second way:
  `HeightmapTerrain.height_at` under the transducer against the `WaterSurface` plane, through the
  terrain list `BaseVehicle` already collects for the wheels — no raycast and no world walk of its
  own. `contains_xz` is the gate, because `height_at` clamps its UV outside the extent and would
  otherwise hand back the edge height as a fabricated bottom. **The transducer's depth is derived, not
  authored**: it rides `-float_depth`, the probe plane, which is both the bottom the buoyancy model
  gives the hull and the exact plane the aground predicate measures — so the sounding passes 0 as
  the bed reaches the probes and the ground bit sets once the bed lifts the hull off its rest
  depth, and the shoal alarm (contract `warn` 1.0, low side) leads both with a metre still under
  her. Only the fore-aft station (`transducer_station`) is a knob; a hand-typed Y would drift per
  variant, each hull having its own `float_depth`. **-1 is the invalid
  reading and the only one** — off every terrain's extent, or out of the water — never 0, which is
  the value an alarm acts on; the `agl` rule, sentinel inside the `range` so the bar and the bridge
  agree on it. It is the one instrument gated on the water: an anemometer reads on the trailer, a
  sounder does not. "Off the terrain" is not the open sea — level 6 is 560 m of water over 512 m of
  terrain, a 24 m ring you have to go looking for.
- `awa`/`aws`/`twd`/`tws` (NMEA 2000 PGN 130306 Wind Data) come from two pure statics on
  `BoatTelemetry`, read out of the same wind vector and hull velocity — measured, not modeled.
  `awa` is 0 dead ahead, positive to starboard, and 0 again when the apparent wind is genuinely
  calm (running dead downwind at wind speed), where the angle is undefined. **`twd` is the
  comes-FROM bearing every marine instrument reads while `WindField.direction_deg` is the
  heading the wind blows TOWARD**; that 180-degree inversion happens in `true_wind` and nowhere
  else. `aws`/`tws` carry no contract `range` on purpose, so they stay off the bar column.
- `BoatTelemetry extends VehicleTelemetry` adds `rudder_actual` (slewed `_steer` as %) and `trim`
  (modeled honest value, like engine_load: `trim_step` chases forward throttle). `pitch`/`roll`
  aren't its own: attitude and height (`pitch`, `roll`, `altitude`, `vspeed`) are shared
  `VehicleTelemetry` fields written by `BaseVehicle._update_telemetry` off the body basis (+ = bow
  up / starboard down), beside the `roll_rate`/`acc_vert` triples; a family publishes them by
  declaring them in the contract, no code. Boat's PITCH/ROLL bars are pure contract metadata.
  `rudder`/`rudder_actual`/`trim` carry the `nmea2000` flavor (PGN 127245 Rudder, 127488 Engine
  Parameters Rapid), and a flavor is half of what earns a bar — `rudder_actual`/`trim` carry no
  `warn` but are ranged, so they are RUDDER and TRIM on the cluster. `pitch`/`roll` stay
  unflavored since they're shared with plane/drone.
- The engine room: `fuel_rate` and `oil_press` (PGN 127489 Engine Parameters, Dynamic) and
  `tank_level` (PGN 127505 Fluid Level) are labelled honest models on `BoatTelemetry`, computed in
  `_tick_extras` ungated by submersion (the gauges read whether the key is at Ignition, not
  whether the hull is swimming). `fuel_rate` keys off `Drivetrain.applied_throttle`, the same
  governed value `fuel`/`coolant` already read, and doesn't reconcile against the abstract `fuel`
  percent, which drains on its own clock. `tank_level`'s fresh/waste ramp and `engine_hours` are
  simpler still — load-independent, gated only on whether the key is at Ignition, the same
  `running` predicate every one of these reads off `BaseVehicle`'s own computation of it.
  `oil_press` is the one place the boat's own `Drivetrain.rpm` is read at all — it drives no
  gearbox limiter here and reaches no other contract signal. `tank_level` is instanced (`count`
  3: fresh water / waste / live-well); the live-well is held rather than modeled, since nothing in
  the sim drives it, and fuel is deliberately not a fourth element — it's already the shared
  `fuel` signal. `engine_hours` (J1939 SPN 247 / N2K PGN 127489) now covers the boat too, and
  dropped its `isobus` flavor to do it — a boat doesn't speak J1939/ISOBUS, so the flavor would
  misstate the wire the reading travels for that family (the `speed_limit`/`wheel_slip`
  precedent); the SPN/PGN pair is a naming reference, not a protocol claim.
- The autopilot (`BoatAutopilot`, PGN 127237 Heading/Track Control) is a **vehicle-level
  controller** in the `DroneModes` shape — pure statics, with `BoatVehicle._autopilot` holding the
  per-tick state — and nothing about it reaches `InputRouter`'s arbitration. Heading hold and
  nothing more: a route would be a whole waypoint system, and this is the teaching object.
  - `nav_mode` (in, STANDBY / HEADING HOLD, the **2** key or sloppyCAN) is a REQUEST;
    `nav_mode_actual` is what the pilot is doing, and the pair is the drone's
    `flight_mode`/`mode_actual` shape. One thing makes them differ: **a hand on the helm.**
    Deflecting `steer` past `HELM_DEADBAND` hands the rudder back for as long as it is held, and
    releasing it re-engages on the NEW heading — so nudging the helm is how you change course
    under the pilot. Not latched, deliberately: a latch needs a release rule of its own, and this
    is what makes the pilot drivable from a keyboard.
  - `heading_cmd` (in) follows the **`rudder`/`guidance_curvature` PRESENCE rule** and it is
    load-bearing here rather than an override: every bearing in [0,360] is legal, so there is no
    "nothing commanded" value to send. Absent means the pilot steers the heading it CAPTURED on
    engage, and only the engage EDGE captures — a bus that commands a course then goes quiet
    leaves the pilot holding it. `heading_target` (out) is that captured course read back, which
    is the only way to see what a locally-engaged pilot settled on; in STANDBY it tracks
    `heading`, the course an engage would take, rather than 0, which is a real bearing.
  - **The pilot steers through the same helm slew the hand does**, and `BoatVehicle` re-runs it
    from its own `_helm` rather than adding a second one. `BaseVehicle` has already slewed
    `_steer` toward the hand by the time `_tick_extras` runs, and re-slewing that value cancels
    exactly — `move_toward(move_toward(x, 0, r), c, r)` is `x` below `c` — so the rudder would
    drift to centre and never advance. Same `spec.steer_speed`, so the autopilot cannot move the
    rudder faster than a helmsman can; `_autopilot` also rewrites `telemetry.steer`, which
    `_update_telemetry` published pre-empted.
  - `KP`/`KD` are derived rather than felt: against each shipped hull's own `rudder_torque` /
    `drag_yaw` / `VehicleMath.inertia_of`, the closed loop lands at zeta 1.6-1.9 on all three
    boats over the whole authority range — overdamped, so there is nothing to limit-cycle at
    60 Hz, and the hulls' own `drag_yaw` already carries most of it. The full derivation is in
    `boat_autopilot.gd`'s header; a hull whose `drag_yaw` is cut needs it re-checked.
- **The playground is level 6's east basin**, generated by `gen_skyport.gd` like the rest of that
  level — a hand edit or a kit-brush stroke there is deleted by the next `scaffold` replay, so all
  three features are generator constants that derive from `SEA_Y`.
  - A **sandbar** (`SHOAL_*`, a round `BrushOps.FLATTEN` stamp in `scaffold`, before the splat
    pass so `classify_splat` paints it sand by height like any beach) crests 0.4 m proud of the
    sea: 31 m of dry crest, and a 20 m blend ring around it that takes the sounding from the
    basin's 5.65 m through the 1.0 m shoal warn to aground over about four seconds at hull speed.
    It is the only thing on the map that makes `depth` a reading rather than a constant — the
    seabed elsewhere is the pan `island_falloff` clamps to 0.
  - A **buoyed channel** (`CHANNEL_*`, `props`) runs 100 m north-south past it at the basin depth,
    its west marks standing where the bar's ring begins. Holding a line of marks with the tide on
    the beam makes `cog` against `heading` a visible crab angle. Cut 14 m inside the west marks and
    you are on the bar.
  - A **measured leg** (`MILE_*`, `props`): 200 m between two gates, east-west, within 20 degrees
    of the tide's own set so the whole drift lands ALONG the track — `stw` against `sog`. Not a
    measured mile — 1852 m does not fit on a 512 m map — but the same instrument.
  - Marks are `buoy` / `buoy-flag` from the watercraft kit, `collision_mode: "none"`, so they add
    no baked body and a boat that hits one is told by the depth rather than by a crash. Their
    recipe aligns them `raw` (the artist's waterline IS the origin), so they are placed by
    `_place_afloat` at `SEA_Y` — `_place`, which stands a measured BASE on a ground Y, is wrong
    for them. Every number above is MEASURED off the sculpted image by `_report_water()`, which
    is a gate rather than a report: it fails the `scaffold` if the bar stops drying, or if the
    fairway, a channel mark or a leg gate ends up in less than `NAVIGABLE_MIN` of water. The
    channel runs 30 m off a bar that dries, so a nudge to either constant would otherwise put a
    mark on the sand and the level would read as authored and sail as a trap.
- **The cluster is chosen, not generated.** The boat declares more ranged flavored "out" signals
  than any other family, and every one of them is a generated bar by default — enough to overflow
  a bar column and spill the cluster into a second one. The hand-built instruments claim their own
  signals through `Dashboard.WIDGET_SIGNALS` (the sibling of `GAUGE_SIGNALS`), which is what keeps
  the boat at eleven rows in one column.
  - A **wind/track rose** (`src/ui/wind_rose.gd`, gated on `awa`/`aws`/`twd`/`tws`/`cog`/`sog`)
    is heading-up: the bow index is fixed at the top and the card turns under it, so the gap
    between the track needle and the bow IS the crab angle. Both speeds gate their angle (`cog`
    reads due north with no way on, `awa` dead ahead in a calm), so each needle is gated on its
    own speed and blanks to `---` at zero.
  - An **echo sounder** (`src/ui/depth_readout.gd`, gated on `depth`) exists for the sentinel: -1
    sits inside a range whose warn is low-side, so the generated bar pinned at the alarm end over
    open water. It blanks to `---` instead, reading the sentinel off the contract's `range[0]`
    rather than a copy.
  - `pitch`/`roll` are in that list too, so the rule reaches the plane and the drone: the
    attitude indicator prints both numbers, and without the exclusion they are also bars.
  - `aws`/`tws` ride the rose; `sog`/`stw`/`current_drift`/`fuel_rate`/`oil_press` ride the
    readout line under the `engine_hours` rule (a running number has no full scale), contract-
    gated like HRS and never duck-typed. `nav_mode_actual` is the PILOT state chip. What is left
    on bars: fuel, coolant, VOLTS, RUDDER, TRIM, SET, TARGET and the three TANKS.
- Every island level's sea is a `WaterSurface`; drive the car in to drown-respawn. The boat is in
  every island roster.

## Plane & drone (flight)

- Protocols are flavors like the tractor's ISOBUS: plane = `canaerospace`
  (`elevator`/`flaps`/`beacon`/`strobe` in, `altitude`/`vspeed`/`flaps_actual` out), drone =
  `dronecan` (`climb`/`arm`/`node_fail`/`flight_mode`/`led`/`hardpoint_cmd`/`gimbal_pitch`/
  `gimbal_yaw` in; `altitude`/`vspeed`/`rotor_rpm`/`armed`, the four instanced ESC signals, pack
  block, node bus, GNSS/rangefinder block, mode/arming block, hardpoint pair, gimbal actuals, air
  data out). Both share the boat's `pitch`/`roll` outs, and both declare the IMU triple
  (`roll_rate`/`pitch_rate`/`acc_vert`) at the cost of three list entries and no code:
  `BaseVehicle` has always computed all three off the rigid body. The plane and the plain `drone`
  are primitive low-poly builds; `drone-mk2`, the drone family's default, is modelled by
  `tools/gen_drone_model.py` (Blender) into GLB parts on the same flight numbers, and draws every
  drone feature on the airframe: per-motor props and wash discs, a gimbal that slews with the
  `gimbal_*_actual` pair, hook jaws on the latch, a spotlight on the `lights` ladder, a
  rangefinder beam to the measured ground, and steady status LEDs — FC (arming state / failsafe),
  GNSS fix, one per ESC (node health) and a four-bar pack gauge.

### Drone: airframe, cargo hook, gimbal

- `DroneVehicle` (`src/vehicles/drone/`) has zero wheels (boat template) and never forks
  `_physics_process`: everything below happens in `_tick_extras`, one fixed order — measure,
  judge, decide, act. That method is 202 code lines against 14-48 for every other family's seam;
  the laws are extracted into nine sibling files, but the orchestration is not. ~18 values cross
  its stage boundaries, so splitting needs a context object (which `WheelDrive` / `TowHost` /
  `RefuseBody` / `TrainSim` share no shape for) or per-tick locals promoted to member state.
  Battery-electric, declares no rpm/gear/fuel/coolant. Each of `drone_propulsion` / `drone_bus` /
  `drone_power` / `drone_sensors` / `drone_modes` / `drone_arming` / `drone_payload` /
  `drone_gimbal` / `drone_air_data` is a pure-static-function file with its own test suite. Left
  on the vehicle itself: the four body laws the mode ladder is built on — `lift_thrust`,
  `heading_frame`, `level_target_up`, `align_torque`.
- The cargo hook is a real mass and centre-of-mass change (`drone_payload.gd`), the drone's
  counterpart of the tractor's three-point hitch. `hardpoint_cmd` closes the latch only with a
  `CargoPayload` inside the capture ray's reach: commanding HOLD over open ground leaves
  `hardpoint_state` false; releasing needs no condition. On latch, mass/COM/inertia/both hover
  collectives recompute, so the craft sags on pickup and leaps on release. `payload_weight` is
  that mass times gravity, in newtons (`uavcan.equipment.hardpoint.Status`'s own unit — a latch
  measures a force on itself). Payloads are level nodes at the level root, never under
  `AuthoringRoot` (a bake input, welded into static chunk geometry — scenery can't be picked up).
- The camera gimbal turns the hood view into an FPV feed (`drone_gimbal.gd`) with no camera code
  at all: `ChaseCamera`'s HOOD mode composes the `HoodCam` marker's whole local transform, so
  writing a basis onto that marker each tick is the entire game side. Both commands are bridge-
  only (the `retarder`/`led` precedent), in degrees not a percent of travel; mount stops are read
  from those signals' own contract ranges at `_ready`. `gimbal_*_actual` chases the command at a
  finite slew rate rather than echoing it.
- The barometer exists to disagree (`drone_air_data.gd`). `altitude` is world/GPS height, `agl` is
  a downward raycast, `baro_alt` is solved from a pressure; a real flight controller fuses the
  three and this bench deliberately doesn't, so all three read different numbers. Two labelled
  models make the gap: the altimeter's subscale is fixed to a standard day while the level's own
  sea-level pressure drifts, and the static port under-reads with the square of airspeed relative
  to the air (position error), so the gap moves flying into level 6's wind.

### Drone: motors and the bus

- Four real motors are the whole architecture (`drone_propulsion.gd`: mixer, spool, thrust curve,
  per-ESC bus, one closed chain). Control laws produce demands (collective, roll, pitch, yaw); a
  quad-X mixer turns those into four normalized motor commands; each motor spools toward its
  command through a first-order lag, thrust applied at that rotor's own scene position. Roll/pitch
  torque fall out of arm geometry; yaw is summed prop reaction torque (`prop_torque_ratio` N·m per
  N of thrust). Mixing is in thrust-fraction space, command is its square root (thrust ~ ω²).
  Saturation is a plain per-motor clamp, no rescaling, no prioritized desaturation: a pinned or
  missing motor costs the craft authority on every axis it contributed to, uncompensated (the node
  failures below are for that). `MOTORS` maps array index to `esc_index`; its sign table is pinned
  against `drone.tscn`'s geometry by test, so a moved rotor fails CI instead of inverting a control
  axis in flight. Motors turn only when armed and key = Ignition; disarming spools down over ~5τ,
  still making decaying lift the whole way — the published `rotor_rpm`, blade spin, vertical
  damper are all gated on the MOTORS rather than the `armed` bit, since `armed` flips in one tick
  and props don't. `rotor_rpm` is the mean of the four `esc_rpm` values.
- The bus is a roster, not a signal list (`drone_bus.gd`). DroneCAN is a network: eight
  independent computers (four ESCs at node ids 11-14, then GNSS/POWER/AHRS/RANGE at 20-23), each
  publishing `uavcan.protocol.NodeStatus` on its own id and able to simply stop. A flat telemetry
  dict can say "the rotors are turning"; only a roster can say "ESC 3 went away." Bit index is the
  roster index, not the node id. The Y key (or bridge `node_fail`) takes one node off the bus at a
  time: an offline ESC's mixer command is forced to zero at the motor (asymmetric authority loss);
  an offline node publishes CRITICAL health, drops out of `node_online`, and its telemetry holds
  at its last value rather than being zeroed — a stale reading is what a node that stopped talking
  actually leaves behind. Health is derived, never injected, so an ESC cooking itself reads
  WARNING with nothing injected at all.

### Drone: flight modes and arming

- The mode ladder (`drone_modes.gd`) — STABILIZE / ALT_HOLD / LOITER / RTL / LAND — is walked by
  the Z key or `flight_mode` over the bridge. A control mode ladder, never a mission system.
  `flight_mode` and `mode_actual` are two signals and their disagreeing is the reading: the first
  is what was asked for, the second what the flight controller is in after every refusal or
  override. Kill the GNSS node during LOITER and the craft drops to ALT_HOLD and says so. Every
  autonomous mode is the same two controllers with different targets: an altitude cascade
  (position → rate → collective, carrying the drone's only integrator) and a PD position
  controller, and neither can out-command the stick since both ceilings derive from the manual
  path. The PD settles at a bounded offset into wind, so the craft crabs and holds a couple of
  metres downwind. A soft geofence (400 m radius, 120 m ceiling, from the arming point) latches
  RTL; changing the requested mode releases the latch. `WorldBounds` is still the hard wall.
- Arming is a decision, not an AND (`drone_arming.gd`). Seven pre-arm checks, one bit each in
  `prearm_fail`: attitude, pack charge, an ESC off the bus, the AHRS off the bus, a deflected
  climb stick, an active failsafe, and (gated on selected mode, ArduPilot's own rule) a missing
  GPS fix. `arming_state` says DISARMED / BLOCKED / ARMED; BLOCKED needs both a powered aircraft
  and a raised switch. Arming is the rising edge of the request; disarming is a level, refused in
  flight, since a switch thrown down while airborne is a five-kilogram brick falling out of the
  sky. The craft stays armed and disarms itself a few seconds after the landed predicate holds.
  Five failsafes rank by severity (MOTOR, BATT_CRIT, BATT_LOW, GEOFENCE, GPS_LOST), top one
  published. `prearm_fail` publishes 0 in flight.

### Drone: wind, pack, sensors

- Wind is a level property (`src/levels/base/wind_field.gd`): a steady base flow plus a
  deterministic gust term (summed sines, seeded, no per-frame RNG, so a flight replays), sampled
  as one horizontal vector. `Level.wind` defaults to null (dead calm). The flight bodies' dampers
  oppose velocity relative to the air rather than the ground, which in still air is exactly the
  old absolute-velocity term (the regression test). Wind is horizontal by construction: no
  updraft, thermal or ridge-lift model.
- The pack (`drone_power.gd`): the drone keeps the shared `battery` out signal, replacing the
  model behind it: 4S LiPo, `V = V_oc(soc) - I * R_internal` over a piecewise-linear OCV curve
  instead of the base's alternator. Alongside ride `pack_current`/`soc`/`pack_temp` (DroneCAN
  `power.BatteryInfo`). `pack_current` is a sum of models (four `esc_current` + a constant avionics
  draw); `soc` coulomb-counts that sum out of a 10 Ah pack (~9.8 min level hover, ~3.9 at full
  climb stick); `pack_temp` is an I²R first-order rise. An empty pack will not arm the motors;
  respawn hands a fresh, cold, full pack.
- The sensors (`drone_sensors.gd`) are the two signals in the whole contract a LEVEL can move,
  both raycasts against the level's own collision, never a scripted volume. `sats` casts sixteen
  rays on a golden-angle spiral over the sky (area-uniform out to an 80° mask angle, a real
  receiver's 10° elevation mask), four per tick round-robin: an unobstructed ray is a visible
  satellite (level 3's building canyons drop the fix to NO FIX while open water reads all
  sixteen). `fix_type` is the DroneCAN `Fix2.status` enum off that count; `hdop` is a labelled
  model of the angular spread of visible rays. `agl` is one ray straight down, -1 the only invalid
  reading (never zero, which a landing detector would act on). GNSS offline publishes an empty
  sky, not a held last value: a fix is a claim about right now. Both rays mask on `Layers.SOLID`,
  omitting `Containment` (the map's boundary walls); including it would eat satellites at the
  coast.

### Drone: what the cluster shows

- `ST_GROUND` is honest on every wheel-less family, each with its own predicate, not shared. The
  base packs the bit from "all wheels in contact" (permanent true on a wheel-less body), so each
  such family overwrites it in `_tick_extras`. Drone's `DroneSensors.landed_now` reads AGL close,
  |vspeed| low, collective demand no more than hover, held for a 0.5 s debounce (slow to set,
  instant to clear); it reads the craft's own AGL so pulling the RANGE node invalidates the
  reading without the aircraft forgetting where it's standing. Boat's `BoatVehicle.aground_now`
  reads mean probe depth against `float_depth` (< `AGROUND_SHALLOW_FRAC` of it) and hull vertical
  speed, on its own debounce ("aground," not "in water"). The two are genuinely different
  arithmetic answering the same question, not one predicate wearing two names —
  `src/vehicles/CLAUDE.md` records a shared predicate was considered and rejected on exactly that
  basis. `VehicleTelemetry.with_status_bit` stamps either over the packed word.
- The IMU triples are complete: `roll_rate`/`pitch_rate` beside `yaw`, `acc_vert` beside
  `accLong`/`accLat` (DroneCAN `ahrs.Solution`'s `angular_velocity`/`linear_acceleration`). Read
  off the rigid body in `BaseVehicle._update_telemetry`, on the shared `VehicleTelemetry`,
  unflavored: any chassis has honest body motion, so another airframe declares them and needs no
  code. Kinematic like the two axes they join: no gravity term, a hover reads 0 not +9.8.
- Two hand-built widgets are instanced only where the vehicle declares their signals. The attitude
  indicator (`src/ui/attitude_indicator.gd`) builds wherever both `pitch` and `roll` are declared
  (boat and plane get it too); conventions are the contract's verbatim and both signs are pinned
  by test, and it OWNS those two signals — `Dashboard.WIDGET_SIGNALS` keeps them off the
  generated bars, which they would otherwise duplicate as a pair beside it. The node strip generates from the instanced `node_health` signal, one square per
  roster entry. Beside them: ESC groups, pack block, SATS/HDOP/AGL. Arm-tip LEDs ride the `led`
  in-signal (packed u32, not a bool — generates no lamp, read off the bridge directly); the buzzer
  is `BeepCommand`.

### Plane

- `PlaneVehicle` (`src/vehicles/plane/`) has three RayWheels (steered nose wheel, two mains) so
  ground roll/takeoff/wheel braking come from the base. `_tick_extras`: prop thrust from
  drivetrain rpm × throttle (the published `rpm` is the number thrust is computed from — modeled,
  since undriven wheels would idle the wheel-derived rpm), lift with a simplified stall fade,
  drag, control torques whose authority scales with airspeed. Steer = coordinated roll+yaw;
  elevator = pitch; flaps slew toward the request.
- All force terms are pure statics through the shared `VehicleMath` dampers (one-tick clamp
  discipline), tested in `tests/test_plane.gd` / `tests/test_drone.gd`. The 60 Hz tick and
  existing clamps are untouched.

## Train & rail

- Protocol is a flavor: `"train"` (in `pantograph`/`doors`; out `pantograph_state`/`doors_state`/
  `catenary_volts`/`motor_current`/`brake_pipe`/`grade`/`coupler_force`, plus the generic
  speed/gear/nav set). No real rail CAN standard is adopted: trains run IEC 61375, not CAN; descs
  credit CiA 421/rail practice as inspiration only. No `steer` (rail-guided); no
  `rpm`/`fuel`/`coolant` (electric). The reverser (N/D/R) rides the existing `gear` byte, so gear-
  owns-direction arbitration is already correct.
- `TrainVehicle extends BaseVehicle` (`src/vehicles/train/`), no ground drive, 6 `gear_ratios`
  kept, only the two seams. Consist is the Kenney bullet loco + wagons at world scale 2.4
  (measured). Self-places on a closed rail loop in `_ready`, ignores `VehicleSpawn` markers.
- Locomotion = a 1D consist sim (`TrainSim`, pure, `tests/test_train.gd`): each car is a mass at
  an arc position on the rail `Curve3D`; per-car grade/Davis resistance/brake, traction on the
  loco only (constant force to base speed, constant power above), spring-damper couplers with
  slack. Couplers/brakes carry the one-tick 60 Hz clamp discipline, reimplemented as their own
  spring-damper force law since a coupler isn't a linear damper, so it isn't a `VehicleMath` call.
  `TrainPlacement.car_pose` (pure) poses each car from two bogie samples `s ± bogie_half_spacing`;
  its basis is right-handed (det +1), guarded by a test — a left-handed basis renders meshes
  inside-out and flips chase views via the hood-camera basis copy.
- The loco is driven kinematically: `gravity_scale = 0`, the sim writes `global_transform` +
  velocities each tick, so `BaseVehicle._update_telemetry` still reads honest motion. A collision
  perturbs the body for one tick, then the sim reasserts the pose (the train plows small props,
  correct for its mass). Wagons are `AnimatableBody3D` followers posed by the same helper.
- Aux systems are honest labelled models in `TrainTelemetry`: `brake_pipe` charges toward 5 bar,
  vents on application; `catenary_volts` = 25 kV nominal minus sag ∝ current; `motor_current` from
  traction; `grade` (i8 %) from the tangent; `coupler_force` (kN) off the sim's head coupler.
  Traction is live only with key in Ignition and pantograph raised; doors open only at standstill.

### Rail runtime

- `RailTrack` (`src/levels/base/rail_track.gd`): rails are authored as a `RoadPath` carrying
  `RailProfile` under `AuthoringRoot`, freed at load and stripped on export, but the train needs
  the `Curve3D` at runtime. The baker emits a runtime-safe `RailTrack` (duplicated curve, gauge,
  closed flag, world transform) per rail road into the baked scene; in unbaked dev play the
  `RoadPath` itself answers the same duck-typed API. Exactly one of the two is ever present.
  `RailTrack.find_closed_rail(root)` is the one shared walk finding a closed loop; both `Level`
  (spawn gate + garage roster gate) and `TrainVehicle` (self-placement) call it, so they can't
  disagree. An open rail is never accepted.
- Dashboard: the train is the only family declaring `gear` out without `rpm`, getting a bespoke
  `REVERSER N/D/R` centre readout in place of the tacho (`docs/systems.md` § Telemetry &
  dashboard). LINE/AMPS/PIPE bars and the request/state lamp pairs are ordinary contract-metadata
  generation.
