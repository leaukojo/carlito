## Decision (finding 8, with the user, same day)

The two sites get different answers. Neither was measured; the reasons are read from the code.

- **Airborne call: pass the body's `slip_floor`.** It reads as an omission: the grounded call
  passes `floor_vel` as its eleventh argument and the airborne one stops at ten, although
  `size_cap_masses` has already given the lifted wheel the body's floor. It also breaks the rule
  `slip_floor`'s own doc states ("one per body ... a driveline that turns the wheels together (a
  centre diff, a TC on the light axle) would hold the heavy ones under theirs"): a lifted driven
  wheel is the lightest there is, and its TC ceiling sits at `0.12 * 1.5` m/s over the road
  where its grounded partners' sits at `0.12 * floor`. The effect is confined to a lifted wheel
  under ABS or TC below the body's floor speed.
- **`road_change`: keep `LOW_SPEED_FLOOR`, reword.** The documented launch and stop figures
  (within 4 % of the 480 Hz reference) were measured with this fade, and no ring is recorded
  between 1.5 m/s and a raised floor. Fading on the raised floor would switch the follow off
  over a wider band on the heavy bodies, which is the part of a launch the doc says the fade
  already costs 1-3 %, and it would re-open every measured launch table.
